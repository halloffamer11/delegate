Ideas tried in this prototype, and why
  • Arm stack. One horizontal arm for each effort, the lowest at the bottom, and each arm is wider than the arm below it. A lane fills its arms green up to its level, so the level reads three ways: count, height and width.
  • Two channels for run and measured. The arm is what runs; a square pip at its right is what the board measured. A solid arm with a solid pip is both. A hatched arm with a hollow pip is run but not measured. An empty arm with a solid pip is measured but not run. Shape and fill carry this, not hue.
  • Identity chip. Hue is the model family (the first word of the model id). The sigil shape and the short name are the model. The fill is the harness: solid, hatched, dashed. One model on two harnesses keeps its hue and sigil; only its fill and its tag change.
  • Tier as signal bars and a digit. Tier has no colour, so it never competes with the identity hue or the effort green. The marks ▲ and ▼ show where a view disagrees with your tier.
  • Pick grid first. The Frontier tab opens with the best lane and the cheapest lane in each tier for each kind of job, because that is the question the page answers.
  • Coverage matrix with one stack in each cell. Each cell shows run against measured at a glance. A line of text marks a view that has no row at an effort you run.
  • Tier table as effort ladders. Lanes sit under their model on a harness, highest effort first, so a tier change between efforts is easy to see. One switch ranks them flat by net tier.
  • View tab as tier columns of cards. Each card has the chip, the stack, a score bar, the cost, flags in words and a table of what each measured effort scores. Order by score, cost or score per dollar; filter by harness; point at a chip to mark that model on every harness. Tables fold into cards below 980px.
