Ideas tried in round 3, and why
  • The plots are the page. Each classification tab draws one plot of score against cost for each of its benchmarks, side by side. A model on a harness is one line, with one dot for each effort the board measured, joined from the lowest effort to the highest. The page opens on the first classification, because the decision is made there.
  • One colour and one shape for each harness. Round 2 gave the hue to the model family. Now the hue and the dot shape both name the harness, and they mean the same in the chips, the plots and the comparison. A label at the end of each line names the model.
  • The frontier is a dark staircase. It joins the points that no other point beats on both score and cost. A point off the frontier is faded. A large filled dot is an effort that a carried lane runs. A small hollow dot is measured only. An outline around a dot is a second harness that runs the same model at that effort.
  • Log cost scale, said on each axis. The costs span several orders of magnitude. A linear scale would put all the cheap lanes on one spot.
  • One consolidated comparison beside the plots. One row for each carried lane, in rank order. The dot is the mean percentile across the benchmarks and the whisker is the spread between them. Each row also has the count of benchmarks that measured the lane, and the tier now against the tier here. It stays in view while the plots scroll.
  • Every mark is linked. Point at a row, a line or a dot, and the same lane is marked in every plot and in the comparison while the others fade. Select it to keep the mark and to open a model card: one dot and whisker for each benchmark across the efforts, and the favoured effort as green slices.
  • Less to read at once. One line of legend. The ranking rule, the scores table, the favoured efforts and the key are collapsed. The Frontier tab is a small overview: the best lane in each tier of each classification, then the tier table with the net tier.
  • Kept from round 2. The ranking rule (mean percentile), the tier cut in the proportions the tiers hold now, the favoured effort, and one CONFIG object for the grouping. All names come from the data.
